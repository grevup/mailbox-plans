The Kid-Mail Express A one-sheet plywood mailbox · all straight cuts

MATERIAL ¼″ × 24″ × 24″ eucalyptus ply SHEETS 1 per mailbox CUTS 9, all straight FASTENERS glue + ¾″ brads

One 2′×2′ sheet makes one complete mailbox — so your two sheets make one for each kid. The roof slopes forward and the front panel stops short, leaving a 1½″ mail slot under the roof overhang. No hole-cutting, no jigsaw: every cut is a straight line.

2Sheet Cutting Diagram

First rip the sheet into three strips (7½″, 6″, and the ~10″ leftover), then crosscut each strip. Leave about ⅛″ between pieces for the saw blade.

✎ Cut order: two long rips first (at 7½″ and then 6″ more), then chop each strip into pieces, angle cuts last.

3The Side Panels — one angle cut

Cut both sides as 6″ × 10″ rectangles first. Then on each one: mark 8½″ up the front edge, and draw a line to the top back corner. Cut the small triangle off. This slope is what tilts the roof.

✎ Make the two sides mirror images — flip one over before marking, or just stack them and cut both at once.

5Assembly — glue every joint, then nail

Use wood glue plus ¾″ wire brads (small thin nails). ¼″ plywood edges are skinny, so: run a bead of glue, hold the piece, and keep nails about ⅜″ in from panel ends so they hit the middle of the edge below.

Make the “U”

Stand the two SIDES up (tall 10″ edges at the back, sloped tops up). Glue + nail the FRONT across their front edges — bottom edges flush, slope gap at the top.

Close the box

Glue + nail the BACK (the 10″ tall piece) across the back edges of the sides the same way. Now you have a tube with a slanted top.

Drop in the floor

Slide the BOTTOM inside so it sits flush with the lower edges (sand its edges a little if snug). Glue, then nail through the front, back, and sides into it.

Roof on, slot made

Lay the ROOF on the sloped side tops — even overhang left/right, about 1″ sticking out past the front. Glue + nail down into both side edges. The gap above the FRONT is the mail slot.

Flag (optional)

Glue the little flag square to the 1″×6″ arm. Attach the arm to a side with one nail, not driven tight, so the kid can raise it when mail is waiting.

Sand, paint, deliver

Round every edge and corner with sandpaper (120 then 220 grit) — eucalyptus ply can splinter. Then hand the kids the paint. Names on the front are mandatory.

Thin-plywood tips: always glue — the nails mostly just clamp while it dries. If an edge starts to split, snip the head off a brad, chuck it in a drill, and use it to pre-drill tiny pilot holes. Practice two nails on the scrap from Strip C first.
